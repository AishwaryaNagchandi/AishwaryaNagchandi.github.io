02 — Outside the Classroom

I don't think a career is built only in classrooms.

Some of my most meaningful lessons have come from places that had nothing to do with exams, assignments or job descriptions — from performing on stage, competing on a court, building ideas with a team, volunteering in my community, listening to music, and simply being curious about the world around me.

Exploring

I am naturally curious, and that curiosity does not always stay within the boundaries of what I am studying.

I enjoy learning about things simply because they make me stop and think — whether that is understanding a new idea, listening to a song on repeat, discovering a museum I have never been to, or going down an unexpected rabbit hole about something I know very little about.

There is always something new to discover.

My interests have taken me from English Olympiads and analytical competitions to music, museums and the arts. Different interests give me different ways of looking at the world — and I think that makes me better at understanding people, ideas and problems.

What ties it together

I like having more than one way to look at the world.

Data taught me to look for patterns. Finance taught me to think about decisions. Sport taught me about competition and consistency. Kathak taught me patience. Music, art and museums remind me that not everything interesting can be reduced to a spreadsheet.

The bigger picture

Work is one part of who I am. Curiosity is the bigger part.

And I think the best work happens when you bring both into the room.
