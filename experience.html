01 — Experience

Different environments.
The same analytical mindset.

My experiences have taken me from a government research institution to my first corporate environment — two settings with very different problems, processes and ways of working.

Learning through research, business problems and people.
02 — The Thread

Two environments.
One analytical mindset.

At IIRS–ISRO, I worked in a research-driven government institution where the focus was on understanding complex scientific and geospatial data, following structured methodologies and approaching problems with precision.


At Dow, my first corporate experience, the problems became more business-oriented — understanding processes, collaborating with stakeholders, structuring information and identifying opportunities for improvement.


The environments were different. The mindset wasn't.

03 / 05

Dow

Management Intern · Integrated Supply Chain
May — September 2026 · Navi Mumbai

What I worked on

My first corporate experience exposed me to the pace, ambiguity and interconnectedness of real business problems.

I worked across process mapping, customer analytics and process improvement — using data to understand how existing processes worked and where opportunities for improvement existed.

Process Mapping Understanding complex business processes and structuring information across different stakeholders.
Customer Analytics Working with multi-region survey data and using Python and Power BI to turn information into interpretable insights.
Process Improvement Studying process quality and identifying opportunities to make workflows more structured and effective.
Stakeholder Management Learning how to ask better questions, communicate findings and work with people from different functions.

What I learned

Dow taught me that the workplace isn't always about having the perfect answer. Sometimes it is about being comfortable asking questions, understanding context and figuring things out as you go.

I also experienced a much more open and approachable environment than I had encountered in academic settings. The people around me were willing to guide, explain and give me the space to learn.

More importantly, I met people from very different professional backgrounds and built friendships that made my first corporate experience genuinely memorable.

Beyond the Job Description

My first experience of what working life actually feels like.

As a student, one of the most valuable things I took away from Dow was understanding that professional growth happens through people just as much as through projects. I learned to be more comfortable navigating ambiguity, reaching out to people, accepting feedback and learning from those with more experience. The openness of the environment — and the friendships I formed along the way — made the experience feel less like simply completing an internship and more like taking my first step into the professional world.

04 / 05

IIRS–ISRO

Data Analytics Summer Intern
May — July 2025 · Dehradun

What I worked on

My experience at the Indian Institute of Remote Sensing introduced me to a very different kind of professional environment — one centred around scientific research, precision and structured processes.

I worked with spectral and geospatial datasets, using Python-based data processing and machine learning techniques to understand patterns within complex scientific data.

Spectral Analysis Processing and analysing spectral datasets across multiple bands and material categories.
Data Pipelines Using Python, NumPy and Pandas to structure and streamline data processing workflows.
Machine Learning Applying analytical and machine learning approaches to classify and interpret scientific datasets.
Research Communication Learning how to communicate technical findings clearly within a research-oriented environment.

What I learned

If Dow taught me adaptability, IIRS–ISRO taught me discipline. The environment was highly structured and precise, with a strong emphasis on methodology, accuracy and following established processes.

Working in that environment taught me the importance of patience and attention to detail. I learned that good analytical work is often less about speed and more about being rigorous enough to trust the result.

I also had the opportunity to interact with people who had deep expertise in their fields. Being around such experienced and knowledgeable professionals gave me a different perspective on what it means to continuously learn.

Beyond the Job Description

Learning how much there is still to learn.

Being a student in a highly structured research environment made me more conscious of the value of discipline. I learned to respect processes, prepare more carefully and pay attention to details that can easily be overlooked. But perhaps the most lasting lesson was personal: meeting people who had spent years building expertise reminded me that becoming good at something is a long process. The experience made me more curious, more patient and more willing to learn from people who know more than I do.
